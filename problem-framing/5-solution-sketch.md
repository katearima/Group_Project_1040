# 5. Solution Sketch

> An outline of a plausible solution. This is not a detailed design, and a visual sketch is optional.


*Author: Ketevan Peranidze*

Our idea is to build a **startup matching platform for university students in the Boston area**, where co-founders looking for teammates and students looking to work at startups can find each other based on shared interests, skills, and preferences.

Instead of scrolling through hundreds of startup listings and trying to figure out which one might be a good fit, students would be able to discover people and ideas that actually match what they are looking for.

**How it would work:**

1. **Register and set preferences.** Both founders looking for co-founders or team members and students looking to work at a startup can register on the platform. Founders can describe their startup idea, what problem they are trying to solve, and what kind of people they need. Students looking to join startups can describe their skills, passions, experience, and what kinds of ideas they would want to work on. Both sides can input preferences such as interests, skills, university, location, and expected time commitment.

2. **Get a personalized feed.** Based on these preferences, the platform would rank potential matches and show users the people who are most likely to be a good fit. Instead of going through hundreds of unrelated listings, users would see founders or builders whose interests and expectations align with theirs.

3. **Swipe through profiles.** Similar to a dating app, users would swipe through profiles of founders and people interested in joining startups. Each profile would show the person behind the idea, their background, and a short pitch or description of what they want to work on. Users could express interest or pass.

4. **Match and connect.** If both people express interest in each other, they match and can start chatting. From there, they can discuss the idea, their expectations, and whether they would want to work together.

**How this addresses the bad situations:**

For Keti, this would reduce choice overload because she would no longer have to go through hundreds of startup names without knowing whether they are relevant to her. Instead, she would see people and ideas that match her interests and skills.

It would also connect students across universities. Keti at MIT could discover Bumba at BU even if they don't attend the same events, belong to the same clubs, or know the same people.

For Bumba, the platform would make it easier to reach students who are genuinely interested in his idea rather than just looking for any startup experience. His pitch would not get buried simply because it was posted earlier or because he doesn't have a large network.

For the initial version, we would focus on registration, profiles, preferences, a simple matching algorithm, swiping, and messaging after a mutual match. The goal is to make it easier for founders and students interested in startups to find people they would actually want to build something with.
