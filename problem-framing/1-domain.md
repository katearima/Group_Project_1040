# 1. Domain

> An explanation of the domain, or general area, in which the problem lies.

**Domain: forming early-stage startup teams among university students in the Boston area.**

## What happens in this domain

Every semester, hundreds of students at MIT, Harvard, BU, Tufts and the other nearby schools either have a startup idea or want to work on one. Before anyone writes code or raises money, a team has to come together, and that is the activity this domain is about. In practice it involves:

- **Founders pitching an idea and recruiting.** A student with an idea writes a short pitch, posts it on their school's listing board, such as MIT Orbit's "Launchpad of ideas and startups", mentions it at club meetings and events, and asks friends whether they know a strong engineer, designer or business partner.
- **Builders looking for something to join.** A student who wants to work at a startup instead of a big-tech internship searches the web, asks people around them, and browses university resources: listing boards, entrepreneurship centers like the Martin Trust Center, clubs, hackathons, pitch nights and accelerator demo days.
- **Evaluating fit.** Both sides read pitches and profiles and try to judge whether the idea, skills, time commitment, values and personalities line up.
- **Reaching out and meeting.** People exchange messages or emails, grab coffee, and often try a short collaboration (a hackathon or a weekend prototype) before committing.
- **Committing (or not).** The pair or team agrees on roles, time commitment and sometimes equity, or they walk away and keep searching.

## Stakeholders and what motivates them

| Stakeholder | What they do | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **Student builders** (engineers, designers, business-minded students) | Search for and evaluate early-stage teams to join | Meaningful impact, learning, ownership or equity, and a team they believe in. They weigh this against the safety and pay of big tech. |
| **Student founders** | Pitch ideas and recruit co-founders and early team members | People who have the missing skills *and* share their passion for the problem, since they usually can't pay salaries |
| **Universities and entrepreneurship programs** (Trust Center, Orbit, incubators) | Host listing boards, events and courses | Successful student ventures and a strong entrepreneurial reputation |
| **Clubs, accelerators and event organizers** | Run pitch nights, hackathons and cohorts | Active, well-matched participants. Some also want strong teams to invest in. |
| **Investors** | Back early teams | Teams that are complete and committed, since team quality is a key early signal |

## Why the setting matters

The Boston area packs many universities into a few square miles, a few T stops apart. The pool of potential founders and builders is unusually large and close together, but each school's startup ecosystem (its listing boards, clubs and events) mostly operates on its own.
