# 6. Ethical Analysis

> Identification of stakeholders and the impacts they might experience in the short and long term, using value-sensitive design (see the course's final-project ethical analysis instructions).


## Stakehoders

### Direct Stakehoders

#### Startup Founders
**Goals** may include: Find fellow founders and founding employees that can share their vision; develop their product; grow their business
**Values**: Growth and long-term prospects of their startup; attention of potential investors and customers; maintaining competitiveness over other startups

#### Prospective Employees and Builders
**Goals** may include: Find a founder or startup with a compelling vision; find a job they can be passionate about; become gainfully employed
**Values**: Various

#### Us
**Goals**: Create and maintain a useful platform; connect startup founders with prospective employees and each other 

### Indirect Stakeholders
Note: for brevity, we omit the indirect stakeholders inherent to all platform-style apps, such as the need for app hosting indirectly increasing demand for data centers.

#### Startups
Startups that do not use our platform will generally have the same goals and values as those who do. If our platform accomplishes its goal and becomes broadly use, startups may feel that they are forced to use it in order to maintain competitiveness.

#### Prospective Employees and Builders
Prospective employees and builders (and other people looking for work) that do not use our platform may suffer in an employer-centric job market. If our platform is highly successful, it may cause some startups to forgo other methods of searching for employees, which could prevent those who do not use the platform from finding those opportunities. In particular, those who value personal connections and more organic networking over the sort of algorithmic matching we will offer may especially suffer as our platform pushes startup culture and hiring further from their ideals.

#### Businesses
**Goals** may include: Hire new employees; retain current employees; maintain business model
**Values**: Growth; stability; profit
Because our platform is not intended for businesses, they will likely be unable to take advantage of it in order to find prospective employees to hire. In an employee-centric job market, this could negatively affect their ability to find people to hire. In the worst case, if they find themselves unable to fill a position that they might have been able to fill if not for prospective employees finding work via our platform, we may have directly hampered their stability and growth. More generally, increasing the share of student and new grad talent that goes to startups decreases the share that can go to established businesses, especially those that might rely on interns and student workers.

#### Entrepreneurship Programs
**Goals** may include: Equip entrepreneurs with skills; connect entrepreneurs with resources and mentors; engender successful businesses
**Values**: Education; Nurturing entrepreneurs
Our platform stands to be helpful towards the goals of entrepreneurship programs, particularly with regard to connecting entrepreneurs with people that might not be regularly on their campus or sphere of influence. However, it would also potentially be in competition with 'in-house' solutions that might put a greater focus on the program's entrepreneurs and startups, elevating them over others, which could put our goals into conflict.

#### Investors
**Goals** may include: Find promising founders and ideas; make money through long-term bets on startups
**Values**: various
Over time, investors may come to certain preconceptions about startups if they learn that they were founded through our platform, for better or for worse. It is difficult to predict the effect that this pattern could have on investor behavior, while could have unexpected consequences for startups and founders.

#### Accelerators, Clubs, Networking Events, Talent Events
**Goals** may include: connect startup founders with prospective employees and each other; connect startups with prospective investors
**Values**: Developing the startup space; finding talent
These stakeholders may welcome our platform with open arms as sharing their goals and missions, or they may see us as potential competitors or even threats to 'traditional' startup culture and networking. Our platform, if successful, would likely disrupt traditional modes of networking that can't provide the benefits of algorithmic matching, which could put us into conflict even if we share most goals and values.

## Time
Over time, our platform could be repurposed or taken advantage of to ends it was not designed for. For example, non-startup companies or corporations could try to use it for recruiting, perhaps by having recruiters make 'founder' profiles for them in order to reach out to prospective employees directly. It's also not hard to imagine the platform being repurposed by groups other than founders looking to find people like them: perhaps someone could try to use it to find people in the area who want to join a chess club they run, or perhaps (in a worst case scenario) someone could use it to find people who might be willing to smuggle narcotics for them. If the platform was big enough, algorithmic matching without oversight could put skulduggerous malefactors in contact with each other, giving them a place to exchange contact information for a more secure platform. 


## Pervasiveness
If successful and pervasive, our platform could fundamentally change how startups are formed and executed on. It might be practically expected that the founding team of a startup was created and matched algorithmically, perhaps to the point of disadvantaging those who meet in other ways by causing people to view them with negative preconceptions.



## Insights

1. Observation: algorithmic matching of a large pool of users could lead to users who are not actually founders or builders using the platform to find each other. Response: add mechanisms to identify and remove profiles that are not indicative of someone looking to found or join a startup, such as a way to report profiles and/or automated analysis of profiles.
2. Observation: entrepreneurship programs may consider the platform to be 'in competition' with them, and may discourage its use over in-house sloutions. Response: add mechanisms to integrate with existing platforms and give users the option to preference matches associated with programs they are a member of.
3. Observation: the platform may 'starve' non-startup businesses of fresh talent. Response: if the platform is big enough and pervasive enough to warrant it, integrations and partnerships with non-startup businesses could be considered to ameliorate the problem by matching prospective employees with them directly.
4. Observation: algorithmic matching is counter to intuitions supporting 'natural' or 'organic' networking and connections, such as through chance meetings or shared event attendance. Response: add mechanisms to integrate with particular events, allowing verified attendees to tag themselves as having attended so matches can bond over having both attended even if they never met at the event.
5. Observation: knowing that a startup's founding team was connected mostly or entirely through our platform/via algorithmic matching could cause people (especially prospective customers and investors) to make assumptions or cast aspersions on the startup, to their detriment. Response: ensure that matches are always private and that there are no publicly accessible logs that could prove that any pair of individuals met via our platform, allowing the users to choose whether or not they wanted to disclose how they met.
